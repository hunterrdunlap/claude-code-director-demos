Same engine, no terminal

Everything you've seen today runs on the same machinery: Claude reads your files, fans work out to a team of helpers, and follows a saved set of instructions. The other demos drive that from a terminal. Claude Cowork drives the exact same engine from a desktop app — no terminal, no commands to memorize. You describe the job in plain English; Claude does the file-reading, the parallel reading, and the writing.

For lawyers, that's the whole pitch: the large-context document work and case-file organization you've watched today, available from an app you already know how to use.

When to use which surface

  • Chat — quick questions, a fast rewrite, "explain this clause." Nothing to set up; nothing saved to your folders.
  • Cowork — autonomous work over real files and folders: digest a 200-page filing, organize a case directory, draft from an organized file. This is where the three workflows below live.
  • Claude for Word — redlining and review inside the document: track-changes edits, clause suggestions, and comments where the writing actually happens.

Rule of thumb: Cowork to read, organize, and draft across many files; Word to mark up the one document you're editing.

What's actually happening: sub-agents

The reason a 500-page case file doesn't overwhelm Claude is sub-agents — think of them as a team of reading assistants. Instead of reading one giant file front-to-back (where the middle gets lost and Claude runs out of room), Claude hands each document, or each section, to its own assistant. They all read at the same time and hand their notes back. A folder of hundreds of files gets read in parallel, not one-at-a-time, and nothing in the middle falls through.

You don't manage the team — Claude spins it up on its own. You just see the result: faster work, and nothing skipped. More on sub-agents →
